Title: The Broken Lute String
Format: TikTok slideshow (7 slides)
Goal: Adaptation.
Style: Traditional Taoist Ink Wash (visual). Script voice: conversational fable—plain language, cause and effect, no lecture tone.

Slide 1 (Intro)
The Broken Lute String.
(A single broken silk string hangs loose from an old lute on a musician's lap, the other strings still taut and waiting. Aged parchment background with soft ink washes.)

Slide 2
A court musician discovers a broken string before a performance and declares the lute useless, refusing to play with imperfection.
He wants every string perfect—but the audience is gathering, therefore pride threatens the music.

Slide 3
The wise traveler (wise old Taoist sage with a long white beard and flowing robes) plucks the five remaining strings and finds a simpler melody that needs no note the broken string would have carried.
The musician thinks the song is ruined—but the melody is clear, therefore less can still be enough.

Slide 4
The musician plays the reduced song in the courtyard and the listeners lean in, hearing a tenderness the full lute never offered.
The broken string changed the piece—but the change deepened it, therefore adaptation became gift.

Slide 5
Adaptation is not pretending the string did not break; it is discovering what the remaining strings can say when you stop mourning the missing note.
The Tao plays with what is left—but we cancel the concert, therefore the music never arrives.

Slide 6
The musician keeps the broken string on the lute as a reminder and plays the simpler song for the rest of his life.
The lute is not whole—but the Tao is the remaining strings, not the missing one, therefore the song continues.
