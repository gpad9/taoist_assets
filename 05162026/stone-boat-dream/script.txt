Title: The Stone Boat and the Dream
Format: TikTok slideshow (7 slides)
Goal: Perspective.
Style: Traditional Taoist Ink Wash (visual). Script voice: conversational fable—plain language, cause and effect, no lecture tone.

Slide 1 (Intro)
The Stone Boat and the Dream.
(A man in traditional robes sleeps peacefully on a mountain ridge. In the sky above him, a heavy boat carved from solid gray stone floats effortlessly on a river of silver light. Aged parchment background with soft ink washes.)

Slide 2
A man dreams of a massive boat carved from heavy gray stone, floating as light as a feather on a river of pure silver light that flows through the stars.
He wakes up and laughs at the "impossible" image, his mind full of the hard rules and heavy weights of the physical world—but the boat is a thought, therefore the weight is an illusion.

Slide 3
The wise traveler watches as the man tries to build the stone boat in the real world, his hands bleeding from the effort of carving the unyielding rock.
He is so focused on the "rules" of what can and cannot float that he has completely forgotten the fluid nature of the Tao—but the stone is hard, therefore his struggle is a wall.

Slide 4
The man eventually finishes his work and pushes the heavy stone boat into the mountain lake, expecting it to sink like a lead weight to the bottom.
To his shock, the boat floats perfectly, its massive weight finally becoming a part of the water's own rhythmic dance—but the Tao is the water, therefore the stone is light.

Slide 5
Your "reality" is always limited by the tools you use to measure it—your beliefs, your fears, and your narrow experiences of the world.
The Tao is the realization that the "stone boat" and the "silver light" are exactly the same thing, seen with different eyes—but we judge the weight, therefore we miss the float.

Slide 6
The sun eventually sets, and the stone boat vanishes into the dark, quiet water, leaving the lake still and the man forever changed.
He walks on into the twilight, his heart feeling light and his path finally clear of the "impossibilities" he once feared—but the Tao is the dream, not the boat, therefore the man is wise.
