Title: The Bronze Mirror Fogged with Breath
Format: TikTok slideshow (7 slides)
Goal: Clarity.
Style: Traditional Taoist Ink Wash (visual). Script voice: conversational fable—plain language, cause and effect, no lecture tone.

Slide 1 (Intro)
The Bronze Mirror Fogged with Breath.
(A bronze hand mirror lies fogged with breath on a cold morning, its polished surface hidden under a soft grey veil. Aged parchment background with soft ink washes.)

Slide 2
A bride breathes on her bronze mirror and curses the fog, convinced her reflection has abandoned her on the morning she needs it most.
She wants a clear face to meet the day—but her breath caused the fog, therefore she fights her own air.

Slide 3
The wise traveler (wise old Taoist sage with a long white beard and flowing robes) wipes the mirror once with a dry cloth and sets it aside, telling the bride to wait until her breathing slows.
The bride thinks waiting wastes time—but the fog is breath, therefore stillness clears what haste cannot.

Slide 4
When the bride sits calmly, the mirror clears on its own and shows a face softer and more real than the one she feared.
Clarity did not require a new mirror—but she had to stop steaming the glass, therefore the reflection returned.

Slide 5
Clarity is not forcing the mirror to show you what you want; it is quieting the breath that fogs what is already there.
The Tao reflects when the air is still—but we panic at the cloud, therefore we miss our own face.

Slide 6
The bride picks up the clear mirror and smiles, no longer needing to check it every heartbeat of the morning.
The wedding begins—but the Tao is the still breath, not the wipe, therefore clarity remains.

Slide 7 (CTA)
Still the breath.
