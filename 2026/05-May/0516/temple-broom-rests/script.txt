Title: The Temple Broom Rests
Format: TikTok slideshow (7 slides)
Goal: Non-attachment.
Style: Traditional Taoist Ink Wash (visual). Script voice: conversational fable—plain language, cause and effect, no lecture tone.

Slide 1 (Intro)
The Temple Broom Rests.
(A simple, handmade straw broom leans against a sun-warmed stone step of a mountain temple. Aged parchment background with soft ink washes.)

Slide 2
The great bronze bell of the temple rings out across the valley, a deep, resonant sound that calls the monks to their morning prayers and sacred duties.
The monks hurry past the broom, their minds full of the "importance" of their rituals and the noise of their own devotion—but the broom is silent, therefore it is already at rest.

Slide 3
The wise traveler watches as the broom remains exactly where it was left, its straw tail catching the soft morning light as it leans against the ancient stone.
It doesn't care about the bell, the prayers, or the status of the temple; it only cares about the simple warmth of the sun and the job it has already finished—but the work is done, therefore the broom is free.

Slide 4
The monks begin their rhythmic chanting inside the hall, their voices low and heavy with the effort of trying to find a peace that they think is far away.
The broom continues to rest in the silence of the courtyard, perfectly at home in the present moment without needing a single rule or ritual to guide it—but the moment is now, therefore the broom is wise.

Slide 5
Sacredness is never found in the noise of your own effort or the complexity of the rules you create to find the truth.
The Tao is the realization that peace is already here, waiting for you to stop being so busy and simply lean against the stone—but we seek the sacred, therefore we miss the simple.

Slide 6
The sun eventually sets, and the monks leave the hall with tired faces, still carrying the weight of their own expectations for the day.
The broom remains in the twilight, its heart light and its path clear, ready to be picked up again when the next morning calls—but the Tao is the rest, not the work, therefore the peace remains.
